Meet Mrs. Marcela

Marcela Laliberte, M.A. School Adjustment Counselor.

Hello! In the school community I'm known as Mrs. Marcela. I'm a Massachusetts School Adjustment Counselor whose professional journey began in Colombia.

For more than 15 years my work has centered on children, adolescents and families across clinical and school settings. Supporting students, educators and families is at the heart of what I do every day.

Every child has a world of possibilities.

One belief has stayed with me through every country, classroom and family I have worked with.

My professional journey

A child's behavior can communicate emotions, experiences and needs they may not yet have the words to explain. Clinical work taught me to listen for that.

  1. 2009

    Bachelor of Science in Psychology

    Universidad Santo Tomás, Colombia.

  2. 2010

    First work with children and families

    The beginning of my professional practice with young people and the adults who care for them.

  3. 2011–2020

    Clinical psychologist in Colombia

    Children and adolescents ages 5 to 18 and their families: mental health, trauma-informed intervention, emotional and behavioral needs, violence-related concerns, family challenges and therapeutic support.

  4. 2022

    School counseling begins

    A move toward school-based work, closer to children's everyday experiences.

  5. 2025

    Master of Arts, Assumption University

    Graduate preparation in school counseling and school adjustment counseling. Licensed Massachusetts School Adjustment Counselor.

Why I chose school-based work

Traditional therapy often meant seeing a child at an appointment and then waiting until the next session. I became curious about what happens in between.

A school lets me observe growth over time, work alongside the adults in a child's daily life and offer support before small challenges grow into bigger ones.

I often think of school as a mini society. It is one of the first places where children learn to navigate relationships, expectations, emotions, differences, successes, disappointments and choices.

  • What happens when a child walks into class frustrated?
  • When a friendship becomes difficult?
  • When they cannot find the words for what they feel?
  • When they succeed at something that felt impossible last week?

My approach

My clinical background helps me understand the complexity behind a child's needs. School-based work lets me turn that understanding into prevention, early support and everyday connection.

  • Social-emotional learning
  • Emotional regulation
  • Behavior support
  • Trauma-informed practices
  • Mental health
  • Communication
  • Relationship-building
  • Prevention
  • Family collaboration
  • Support for educators

Children need our presence.

  • Adults who listen
  • Who notice and validate
  • Who remain consistent
  • Who believe in their possibilities, even on hard days

Children change, families change, schools change. I keep learning and adapting so I can meet children where they are.

A multilingual, multicultural view

I'm originally from Colombia and I speak Spanish, English and Portuguese.

To understand a child, we must also be willing to understand the world around that child. Culture, language, family experiences, school and community all shape how children communicate, learn, connect and respond.

Why I created this space

This site brings together my background in psychology, school counseling, social-emotional learning, prevention, creativity and multicultural experience.

I love turning important ideas into experiences children can understand: crafts, visuals, activities, stories and practical strategies. Here I share approachable resources for students, educators, counselors and families, to support children as they learn and grow every day.
